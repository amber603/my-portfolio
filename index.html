Case Study: Youth Camp Activity Engagement Analysis

Project Background

This case simulates a two-week youth camp project. 39 participants (ages 14-18, diverse genders and nationalities) rated their engagement (on a 1-5 scale) across 6 core activities at the end of Week 1 and Week 2.

Following Week 1, the project team made several micro-adjustments to the activity execution based on engagement feedback (e.g., adding a movie night voting option). This analysis focuses on the quantitative comparison between Week 1 and Week 2 to evaluate the overall trend in engagement changes, without attributing causality to specific adjustment strategies.

Analysis Objectives

  • — Overall Trend: Did engagement significantly improve in Week 2 compared to Week 1? What was the magnitude of this change?
  • — Individual Consistency: Is this improvement concentrated among a few participants, or is it a widespread trend across most individuals?
  • — Statistical Reliability: Does the data meet the assumptions for a paired-samples t-test? Are the conclusions statistically robust?

Statistical Validation & Assumption Testing

Before conducting in-depth visual analysis, this study first performed normality and significance tests on the data to ensure the business conclusions have solid statistical backing.

Normality Test (Shapiro-Wilk)

To verify the prerequisite assumptions for parametric testing, a distribution test was conducted on the difference scores of each participant.

Result: p = 0.168 (> 0.05) The difference data follows a normal distribution; the paired t-test is appropriate.

Paired-Samples T-Test

Compared the overall mean difference of all participants between Week 1 and Week 2, ruling out random error.

Result: t(38) = 25.49, p < 0.001 The increase in engagement scores between the two weeks is highly significant.

Interpretation: The extremely low standard deviation of differences indicates that the magnitude of improvement is highly concentrated around the mean, with minimal individual variance. This result cross-validates the significant improvement at the group level.

Research Insights & Next Steps

Based on the comparative analysis of engagement between Week 1 and Week 2, this study confirms a significant improvement at the group level and high consistency at the individual level, providing the following directions for subsequent user research:

01

Qualitative Attribution

It is recommended to conduct semi-structured interviews with participants in the top 20% and bottom 20% of engagement improvement after Week 2, to uncover specific factors.

02

Quantitative Breakdown

Collect detailed ratings for each activity in follow-up research to identify high-contribution and low-contribution activities, establishing an impact matrix.

03

Early Warning Mechanism

Establish an "At-Risk Participant Warning List" after Week 1, utilizing brief surveys to proactively identify individuals and implement timely interventions.
